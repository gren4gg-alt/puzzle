How to reduce media size without guessing

A repeatable comparison process for image quality, audio bitrate, video resolution, and destination compatibility.

Start with where the file is going

Before compressing, identify the destination’s accepted formats, size limit, and purpose. A photo for a small chat preview has different needs from a photo someone will print. A voice recording needs intelligible speech; a screen demonstration needs readable text. The smallest possible file is rarely the only useful goal.

Keep the source as a master and make comparisons from that source. Give exports clear names that include the setting you tried. This avoids accidentally comparing a fresh export with a file that has already been compressed several times.

Compare images one setting at a time

Begin with output dimensions. If a photo is much larger than its intended display, reduce its longest side while preserving its proportions. Inspect the result at the size someone will use it, then check important details more closely. Do not discard detail needed for cropping, printing, or reading small text.

Next compare formats the destination accepts. Try JPEG for a photograph, or PNG when lossless graphics or transparency matter. WebP is another option if the receiving application supports it. Measure the outputs rather than assuming one format always produces the smallest file.

Finally adjust lossy quality gradually. Look for blocky edges, smeared textures, color banding, and damage around letters. If one lower setting saves only a small amount but visibly harms the subject, keep the previous version. PNG’s lossless encoding does not respond to the quality slider like JPEG or WebP.

Listen to the hard parts

For spoken audio, compare a lower bitrate with a middle setting. Listen to quiet words, consonants, background noise, and any music. A clean opening sentence is not enough to judge the entire file. For music, compare busy sections as well as simple ones.

Broshere’s audio tool records in real time and exports WebM audio, generally with Opus. Check that the destination accepts that format before spending time processing a long recording. A site that requests MP3 specifically may reject a perfectly playable WebM file. Changing the extension does not solve that mismatch.

Judge movement and text separately

Video resolution determines the size of each frame; bitrate influences how much encoded information is available over time. A low resolution can make small text unreadable even when the bitrate is generous. A low bitrate can damage fast motion even when the frame dimensions remain large.

Compare a short source clip at 720p and 480p if both suit the destination. Look at moving subjects, camera pans, detailed backgrounds, and any text overlays. Listen for missing audio or synchronization problems. Test the downloaded file outside the tool, including a point near the end.

The browser’s recording features and supported codecs determine which sources can be processed. Keep the tab active during real-time video compression. If a long clip fails, first test a short sample in the same format so you can separate a support problem from a resource or duration problem.

Use size estimates as a starting point

Bitrate multiplied by duration gives an approximate amount of encoded data. Divide bits by eight to estimate bytes, then allow for audio tracks and container overhead where relevant. Actual output can differ because encoders do not always follow a requested target exactly.

An already compressed source may grow when encoded with more generous settings. If the tool reports little saving or a larger result, keep the original when it already meets the destination’s requirements. Compression is useful only when the result improves the practical tradeoff for your use.

Keep a simple comparison record

For each candidate, note the output format, dimensions or bitrate, measured file size, and the detail you inspected. For example: “JPEG, 1280-pixel maximum, quality 75; face and text remain clear.” Compare that with one lower setting. This makes your choice repeatable without requiring a technical quality score.

For a fixed-size application photo, use the dimensions checklist. For a Photo Puzzle Exchange download, do not compress it at all: editing can remove the information needed to solve it. Follow the puzzle sharing guide instead.
