Dune Runner guide: routes, hazards, and shard chains

Read the desert, choose safe routes, and learn the distinct responses to logs, walls, boulders, and falling rocks.

Find your line through the desert

Dune Runner moves you forward automatically through a desert valley. You control the route across it, when to jump, and how to respond to the obstacles ahead. Survival and scoring overlap, but they are not identical goals: collecting shards and maintaining a chain can produce a different score from simply travelling a long distance.

Before your first run, open How to Play. The tutorial explains the inputs for your device and shows the three special shard effects. Start by learning to pass one obstacle at a time. Once you recognize the different shapes, you can begin linking safe lines together and collecting shards on the way.

Steering and movement

On a keyboard, use A and D or the left and right arrows to steer. W or Space jumps. Hold S or the down arrow to brake on the ground; pressing S in the air brings you down with a slam. M controls sound and Escape pauses. Braking gives you more time to choose a line as the run speeds up, at the cost of travelling more slowly.

On a touch device, drag across the screen to steer and swipe up to jump. A downward swipe in mid-air slams you back to the sand. The settings menu also offers gyro steering on supported devices. Tilt uses the phone’s motion; Tilt+Swipe combines it with the sideways flick dodge. Motion permissions and sensor support depend on the device and browser.

Use the gear menu to adjust steering sensitivity. If a small gesture throws you across the valley, lower the sensitivity and try another run. If you cannot reach openings in time despite starting early, try a modest increase. Change one setting at a time so that you can tell whether it helped.

Match the response to the hazard

Logs: jump across

A log spans the valley, so trying to squeeze around an end is the wrong approach. Prepare a jump and look beyond the log for the next landing area. Jumping too soon can put you back on the ground before the obstacle arrives; jumping without checking beyond it can set up the next collision.

Walls: steer to the opening

Stone and block walls leave a gap and are too tall to jump. Find the opening early and begin aligning with it while you still have space. If you wait until the wall fills the screen, a large correction may not finish in time. Use braking on a keyboard when you need more time to read the opening.

Rolling boulders: go around

Boulders move across the valley and turn at the walls. Watch their direction as well as their current position. Do not try to jump over them: passing over the top can trigger a blast. Choose a route around the boulder and leave room for its sideways movement.

Storm rocks: leave the red ring

A red ring marks where a rock is about to land. It targets the line you were using when the warning appeared. Move away from the marked patch early instead of waiting for the falling rock itself. Once you have left the ring, look for the next warning rather than immediately returning to the same line.

Reading two obstacles together

Imagine a wall opening on your right followed by a log further ahead. First align with the wall opening. Keep the jump available while passing through it, then jump the log when it approaches. Jumping toward the wall accomplishes little because the opening is a sideways routing problem.

Now imagine the same wall with a rolling boulder nearby. Recheck the boulder’s travel before choosing your final approach to the gap. Shards can help reveal a route, but the next moving hazard still needs your attention. A route is a sequence of decisions, not a promise that one steering input will remain safe.

Shards, chains, and protection

Shards are placed along routes through obstacles. Collecting them extends your chain and raises the multiplier; an impact breaks the chain. Passing close to an obstacle can score as well, but chasing a close pass is a poor trade if it causes a collision. Learn a comfortable route before trying to make it tighter.

The tutorial identifies three special effects: Ward absorbs an impact, Surge lets you smash through while active, and Draw pulls shards toward you. Watch the on-screen effect indicators. Temporary protection changes your options only while it is active; it should not replace reading the next hazard.

Compare both distance and score after a run. A longer run with a broken chain may not beat a shorter run with better collection. To practice scoring, take a route you can already survive and improve how consistently you collect its shards. To practice survival, let an awkward shard go and preserve your line.

Performance and saved progress

This is a 3D game, so graphics performance varies more between devices than with a simple word board. Lower the quality setting if movement feels uneven, close other demanding tabs, and allow the initial game assets to load. Smooth movement makes timing easier to judge; visual quality can come second while practicing.

Records, coins, wardrobe choices, and supported settings are stored in this browser. They are not synchronized through a Broshere account. Clearing site data, changing browsers, or playing privately can affect persistence. In-game coins are part of the game’s progression, not a cash balance.

Why does tilt steering not respond?

Check the selected steering mode and any motion permission prompt. Some devices do not expose the sensors the game needs. Drag steering remains the alternative on touch devices. Avoid changing orientation or grip mid-run while learning tilt; establish a comfortable neutral position first.

What should I report if the game fails to load?

Include your device, browser, the stage where loading stops, and whether the problem also occurs after refreshing. If only a costume is missing, identify that costume. This is more useful than a general “game broken” report because the world and optional models load through different parts of the game.
