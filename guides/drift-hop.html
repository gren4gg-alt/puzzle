Drift Hop guide: timing and all eight levels

Guide Momo to the portal by reading moving platforms, planning landings, and learning each level’s movement pattern.

Start with the landing

Drift Hop is the platform game reached through the Ragebait level selector. You guide Momo over moving islands to a glowing portal. Falling is part of learning the route: the game records falls, while completing a level advances your progress. Finishing safely with fewer falls is a more useful first goal than rushing every jump.

Use A/D or the left and right arrows to move and Space to hop. On touch devices, use the left, right, and HOP controls. P pauses, H opens help, M toggles sound, and F toggles fullscreen. The top controls also provide these actions. Pause before taking a break; leaving a moving platform unattended during live play can cost a fall.

The next island’s current position is only half the information you need. Watch its direction and ask where it will be when you land. A platform travelling toward you may offer a better jump a moment later; a platform moving away may leave the landing area before you arrive. Wait on a stable foothold whenever the level gives you one.

A repeatable timing drill

At a difficult gap, watch one complete movement cycle without jumping. Pick a visible reference, such as the next platform rising level with your feet. Jump on the following cycle at that reference and observe whether you arrive early, late, too far left, or too far right. Change one part of the attempt rather than changing the whole approach.

For example, if you pass over an island while it is still below you, the useful adjustment may be waiting for it to rise further before takeoff. If it moves out from under you, adjust for its direction instead of simply holding the movement button longer. Try to land near the middle so that you have room to prepare the following jump.

Level 1 — Drift Hop

The opening level introduces sky islands that travel up and down on independent paths. They do not all arrive at a convenient height together. Stand on your current island, watch the next one approach a usable height, and make one deliberate hop. Reaching a platform is a chance to stop and observe again.

The common mistake is treating the islands as a staircase. The route may resemble stairs in one instant and become a series of large gaps a moment later. Learn one transition at a time and resist jumping immediately after every landing. Play level 1.

Level 2 — Bounce Pads

Bounce Pads changes the rhythm: landing launches you again. You cannot use the same long pause on every surface that worked in level 1. Begin choosing the next landing while you are still airborne, and use horizontal movement to guide the bounce toward it.

Think in pairs of landings. Before arriving at a pad, locate both that pad and the next one. Aiming for the center reduces the amount of correction needed after the automatic launch. If a chain of bounces fails repeatedly, identify the earliest landing that sends you off line instead of blaming only the last miss. Play level 2.

Level 3 — Orbit Run

Platforms now sweep around circular paths. A platform can be close enough horizontally but still moving upward or away from your landing height. Watch the full loop and identify the part that brings it into reach. Riding a platform further around its orbit may create an easier transfer than jumping as soon as the next one appears nearby.

The key question becomes “where will the circle carry me next?” Stay aware of your platform’s own motion as well as the target’s. When you miss, compare the two positions at takeoff and landing. Repeating a jump at the same wrong phase will usually repeat the same problem. Play level 3.

Level 4 — Mountain Drift

The drifting-island pattern returns above a mountain valley, with longer travel and wider gaps. Use the observation habit from the first level, but allow more time to learn the larger movement. A familiar-looking platform does not imply a familiar jump window.

Look for moments when your current island and the next one reduce the gap together. Jumping when only one looks well placed can leave the other carrying you away from the route. Reset your rhythm at each landing and keep the final portal in view without skipping the intermediate timing checks. Play level 4.

Level 5 — Valley Pendulums

Pendulum platforms travel along swinging arcs. They linger around the ends of a swing and move quickly through the lower part. Their speed therefore changes during the cycle. A distance that looks manageable near the bottom may disappear much faster than expected.

Observe a full swing before committing to a difficult transfer. The slower part can give you more time to judge a landing, but you still need enough horizontal and vertical reach. Learn where the useful window occurs for each pair of platforms instead of assuming every jump should happen at the same end of an arc. Play level 5.

Level 6 — Mountain Orbit

Ten orbiting platforms carry the route along the ridgeline. Use the circular-motion skills from Orbit Run and treat each transfer as a fresh phase-matching problem. Riding a loop is often more useful than forcing a jump while the next platform is receding.

Longer sequences make it tempting to rush after a successful opening. Keep checking the next orbit even when the first few transfers feel easy. A calm landing near the middle gives you a better starting position for the next decision than an edge landing followed by an immediate panic jump. Play level 6.

Level 7 — Summit Drift

Twelve islands and long swings extend the drifting-platform challenge near the summit. The route rewards consistency across many landings. Use the same small observation routine each time: direction, gap, landing position, then takeoff.

If you keep failing late in the level, slow down the decisions before that point. A poor landing one platform earlier can force an awkward next jump. Try to improve the setup for the difficult gap, not just the final jump itself. Completing a clean opening repeatedly is meaningful progress even before the full route is finished. Play level 7.

Level 8 — Pendulum Peak

The final level combines ten swinging arcs with long travel and tight timing. Bring back the distinction between slow swing ends and fast lower passes. Watch each target through enough of its arc to judge where it will be when Momo arrives.

Plan the last few transfers as carefully as the first. Seeing the portal can encourage a premature hop, but finishing still depends on a safe approach. If a timing attempt fails, keep a useful reference from it: a platform’s height, swing direction, or the point at which the gap begins closing. Play level 8.

Progress, retries, and common questions

The selector stores cleared levels and best fall counts locally. Reset Progress removes that saved progress in this browser; it is intended for starting the sequence again. There is no account-based recovery or synchronization. A browser with blocked or cleared storage may not retain the same progress when you return.

The selector reports total falls from the saved level records, so read it alongside each level’s best result rather than as a complete history of every attempt ever made. Use Retry for another attempt at the current level and Home to return to level selection.

Do I have to memorize every jump?

Memorizing a useful visual cue helps, but understanding the motion is more flexible. Drift, bounce, orbit, and pendulum levels each reward a different kind of observation. Start with their movement pattern, then learn the individual gaps.
