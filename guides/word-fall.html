How to play Word Fall: words, time, and combos

Build words from falling letter tiles, manage the countdown, and learn exactly how scoring works in Broshere’s Word Fall.

Your first round

Word Fall is a word-building game with a moving board. You choose individual letter tiles to spell a word, then submit it before the countdown empties. You are not copying a whole word falling down the screen. The useful skill is spotting words in the letters available right now, while leaving yourself enough time to make the next decision.

Begin with Easy and the All category. Easy starts with 25 seconds, Medium with 20, and Hard with 15. Higher settings also bring faster letters and more columns. Press Play, scan for a familiar three- or four-letter word, and make one successful submission before trying to build a long word. The time bar matters more than a perfect opening score.

Choosing and submitting letters

On a phone or tablet, tap letter tiles in the order you want them in your word. Your selection appears in the word bar below the board. Use the backspace button to remove the last selection, the cross to clear the selection, and GO to submit. You can combine tiles from different parts of the board; they do not need to touch each other.

On a keyboard, type the letters you can see, then press Enter. Backspace removes the last letter and Escape clears your current word during play. A typed letter needs a matching available tile: the keyboard does not create extra letters. If you need two copies of a letter, you need two tiles. Use the pause and fullscreen buttons for reliable shortcuts. P, F, and H also work as pause, fullscreen, and help shortcuts when there is no matching available letter tile; when that tile exists, the letter selection takes priority.

Scoring, with a worked example

Selections contain three to seven letters. A valid word must contain at least three letters and appear in the game’s word list. Its score is the number of letters multiplied by the current level, 10, the length bonus, and the combo multiplier. Three- and four-letter words have a length bonus of 1; five- and six-letter words use 2; seven-letter words use 3.

The combo starts at 1, increases before each successful word is scored, and tops out at 8. Each valid submission refreshes a six-second combo window. For example, at level 1, your first valid CAT scores 3 × 1 × 10 × 1 × 2 = 60 points. If you then submit CATS inside the combo window, with those tiles available, it scores 4 × 1 × 10 × 1 × 3 = 120 points.

Valid words also add 1.8 seconds per letter, up to 10 seconds for one word. The timer cannot rise above the round’s maximum. CAT can therefore add up to 5.4 seconds, but adds less if the bar is nearly full. An unrecognized word of at least three letters costs two seconds. A selection shorter than three letters prompts you to add more letters.

Every five valid words advances the level, increasing falling speed and the rate at which letters arrive. Higher levels also increase the level factor in scoring. A new level therefore changes both the pressure and the points available.

Keeping a round alive

When time is short, submit a word you already recognize. Spending your last few seconds searching for a seven-letter word can lose a round that a simple three-letter word would save. When the timer is comfortable, look for endings such as S or ED that might extend a word, but check that the complete word is accepted and that all its tiles are present.

Keep scanning beyond your current selection. If you see CAT and also enough letters for TREE, decide which one is easier to finish first, then recheck the board after submission. A short sequence of correct words can build a useful combo. Do not assume the second word is still available after tiles have moved or been removed.

The category buttons include Animals, Food, Nature, Sports, and Tech. In this version, a chosen category supplements the general word list; it does not reject every word outside that topic. All is the simplest setting while learning. The dictionary is a game resource rather than a complete authority on English, so a real word can still be absent.

Using the three power-ups

Bomb removes all unselected letters while keeping the tiles in your current selection. Freeze stops falling letters and pauses the countdown for five seconds, giving you a breathing space. Shuffle changes unselected letters. Watch the count beside each power-up: a button with no remaining uses cannot supply a rescue.

Use Shuffle when your unselected letters offer poor combinations, rather than while you are still deciding which letters to keep. Freeze is useful when you already see a promising word but need a moment to finish it. Bomb can clear clutter, but it also removes unselected tiles you may have wanted for the next word. Re-read the board after any power-up instead of submitting a word from memory.

Common questions

Why was my word rejected?

Check the word bar for the actual spelling, confirm the length, and try a common alternative. Word Fall loads an additional word file and has a smaller built-in fallback if that file cannot load. A network or hosting problem can therefore affect which words are recognized. Include the word and selected category when reporting a missing entry.

Why did my combo drop?

The combo window expired without another successful word. Clearing your selection does not itself earn points or refresh the combo. Concentrate on maintaining accurate submissions rather than repeatedly pressing GO.

What should I practice next?

Play several Easy rounds with the goal of avoiding invalid submissions. Then practice building a combo before moving to Medium. Scores from different difficulties and levels involve different conditions, so compare runs made with the same settings when judging improvement.
