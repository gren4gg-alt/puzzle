How to play ROLL: the Ball Runner guide

Learn jumps, double jumps, ducking, shields, and timing in Broshere’s landscape endless runner.

What a successful run looks like

ROLL, listed as Ball Runner in the game hub, is an endless survival game. The ball moves through a changing course, and your distance is your score. There is no final level to finish. Your immediate goal is to pass the next hazard safely; your longer goal is to improve your best distance through better decisions.

The game uses a landscape playfield. On a phone, turn the device sideways before starting. This gives you room to see incoming hazards and reach the touch controls. Press Start Run once you have located the jump, duck, and movement controls. For the first attempt, watch how long a normal jump lasts before trying rapid double jumps.

Jumping and ducking solve different problems. A jump changes your height above the ground, while ducking reduces your profile for a low passage. Horizontal movement changes where the ball is on the playfield; it does not let you leave the course. Learn each action separately before combining them in a difficult sequence.

A practical jump sequence

Suppose a ground obstacle approaches with open space beyond it. Keep watching the gap between the ball and the obstacle, then make one jump as it comes into reach. Check where the ball will land before spending the second jump. A second press can extend the time you stay clear of a hazard, but it also changes when you return to the ground.

Pressing both jumps immediately often wastes the second one. A better practice drill is to use a normal jump, observe its arc, and add the second press only if the obstacle or landing requires it. There is no single safe delay for every situation: the course speed and the next hazard both matter.

If the next obstacle requires ducking, a long airborne sequence can leave you poorly placed to respond. Read two hazards together when you can. Clear the first with the smallest action that works, then prepare the input for the second. This is especially useful once the course begins moving faster.

Warnings, biomes, and shields

The biome label tells you which environment you are passing through. Different environments bring different hazards. Treat a change of scenery as a cue to look carefully at the upcoming course, rather than assuming the same movement pattern will work indefinitely.

Red warning flashes signal an incoming danger such as a spear or obstacle. They are a prompt to check the game area, not an instruction to jump automatically. Identify where the threat will arrive and choose a movement that clears it without placing the ball in the path of another hazard.

Shield pickups let you survive an additional hit. The SHIELD badge in the upper part of the playfield shows when protection is active. After an impact, check the badge again. Do not plan the next risky move on the assumption that protection remains. A shield is most useful as insurance while you continue trying to avoid collisions.

A better way to practice

Give each short session one goal. In one run, focus on learning normal jump timing. In the next, try to keep your second jump available until you need it. In another, pay attention to the warning flashes and name the hazard before choosing a response. This makes even a short failed run informative.

After losing, remember the action immediately before the collision. Jumping too early, pressing the second jump without looking, and moving toward an obstacle while watching only the ball are different mistakes. Change one of those decisions in the next attempt. Repeating all your inputs faster rarely solves a timing problem.

Your best-distance display gives a simple reference for later runs. Local saved records belong to this browser and device; they are not an online leaderboard or an account backup. Clearing site data or using another browser can change what record you see.

Troubleshooting and questions

Why do I see “Rotate your device”?

ROLL intentionally uses a landscape layout. Turn your phone or tablet sideways and check whether the operating system has rotation locked. On a desktop, use a browser window wider than it is tall. You can read this guide in either orientation.

Why are there no touch buttons on my laptop?

The touch controls are shown for devices detected as using a coarse pointer without hover. Use the keyboard controls on a normal desktop. Some hybrid devices report input capabilities differently; a connected keyboard can be the more reliable option there.

Can I stop without losing my run?

Use Pause, then Resume when ready. Returning Home or reloading the page leaves the active run. Sound can be muted separately, so turning off audio does not require ending the game. The short in-game How to Play panel remains available for quick reminders.
