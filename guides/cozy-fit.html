Cozy Fit puzzle guide: shapes, rotation, and smart hints

Fill the silhouette, keep the holes clear

Cozy Fit is a packing puzzle built around colorful pieces and a pale board. Your goal is to fill every playable cell with the supplied pieces, without overlapping them or placing any part outside the silhouette. The shapes range from a simple rectangle to boards with narrow arms, cutouts, and internal holes.

The pale area is the puzzle. Empty space around it, and intentional holes inside a shape, are not missing cells that need filling. Read the outline before you move a piece. A U-shaped or picture-frame board can be solved only if its central opening remains empty.

Begin on the Cozy Trail

The home screen presents 50 levels on a winding map. Continue opens your highest unlocked level. Completing a puzzle opens the next stop; cleared levels remain available for replay. The glowing marker shows where you can continue, while the check marks identify completed puzzles.

The first two levels teach movement without rotation. Level 1, Hello, Little Blocks!, uses four large square blocks. Level 2, First Mix, introduces different pieces facing the right way. Level 3, Arrow Garden, unlocks rotation and a shaped outline. Later levels add tighter pockets and more pieces that seem to fit in several places.

The second half of the trail includes recognizable silhouettes such as a fish, a turtle, a shield, a planet, and a cat. The pictures are a way to organize spatial constraints: a narrow tail or a one-cell bridge may determine a useful placement more strongly than a large open central area.

Move, select, rotate, and remove

Use a mouse, touch screen, or stylus to drag a piece from its parking spot toward the board. The preview helps you align it, and a legal placement snaps into position. A piece must fit entirely within available cells. Merely releasing it close to the outline does not make an overlapping placement valid.

A tap or click without dragging selects a piece and gives it an outline. Tap it again to deselect it, or choose another piece. From level 3 onward, select a piece and use Rotate or press R. Finish a drag before trying to rotate. Rotation turns the shape; the game does not provide a separate mirror-flip control.

Placed pieces are movable. Pick one up and move it to another legal position, or drag it outside the puzzle and release when DROP TO REMOVE appears. It returns to its parking spot. This is how you reopen space after discovering that an earlier move blocked the remaining pieces.

Help me or H asks for guidance. Restart returns you to a fresh arrangement of the current puzzle. The home icon or Cozy Fit logo returns to the map. F and the fullscreen button change the display; sound effects and background music have separate controls. Settings also includes Reduce motion.

Work through a small packing decision

On the first board, notice that a 4 × 4 rectangle is being filled by four 2 × 2 squares. Placing a square in a corner leaves room for three more squares. Placing one across the center may be legal locally but makes the remaining spaces harder to cover with the same square pieces. Legal now does not always mean useful later.

This distinction becomes the central challenge on later boards. Suppose you have a three-cell straight piece and a narrow corridor that only a straight piece can cover. Try reserving that piece for the corridor before using it in the roomy center. Then inspect what shapes remain in the open area.

Count cells in a small leftover pocket. If it has two cells but all your remaining pieces need three or more, something earlier must change. Before restarting the whole puzzle, remove the neighboring piece that created the pocket and try a different orientation or position.

What Help is telling you

The hint system checks whether your current arrangement can still lead to a complete solution. When it finds one, it highlights a safe next placement. This is more flexible than comparing your moves with only one stored answer: an alternative valid arrangement can still receive a useful next move.

If the board has no completion, Help tries removing placed pieces one at a time to find a move that restores a solution. A highlighted blocking piece is therefore a suggestion to reopen that part of the board. Remove or reposition it, then look again at what the freed space makes possible.

The search has limits. When it cannot finish a search or find a single-piece repair, it can suggest a piece to reconsider instead of proving that piece is the only mistake. Read the message: a confirmed dead end and a general suggestion to untangle the board are different kinds of help.

Try using a hint as a question: why does that piece fit this pocket, and what space does it leave for the others? After understanding the clue, continue without requesting another immediately. You will learn more from the shape relationship than from copying a succession of glowing placements.

A strategy for the harder silhouettes

Start with restrictive regions: narrow ends, deep corners, and small branches. Compare those with the pieces that have the fewest plausible orientations. Broad empty rectangles can often accept several pieces, so committing there first may consume a piece needed somewhere more specific.

Check the leftover space after every few placements. A neat-looking row is not enough if it splits the remaining board into unusable pockets. When stuck, undo your most recent assumption by removing a piece rather than continuing to force every remaining shape into an impossible space.

Rotation changes the available possibilities without changing a piece’s area. A five-cell piece always covers five cells. On boards with holes, inspect both the outside boundary and the inside edge; a shape can match one while protruding across the other. Use the placement preview to check both before release.

Moves, stars, and saved progress

The game displays moves and elapsed time, then records a best time for completed levels in the current browser. Stars are based on the move count compared with that level’s internal par: at or below par earns three stars, within six additional moves earns two, and a longer solution earns one. Finishing the puzzle still unlocks progress regardless of the star count.

For learning, aim for a complete solution before chasing a time record. Replay a familiar board to reduce unnecessary placements and removals. Comparing times while trying to learn a new silhouette can encourage rushed choices that create more work.

Unlocked levels, best times, and sound, music, and motion preferences use local browser storage. They are not synchronized through a Broshere account. Clearing site data or switching browsers can remove access to the same saved record. The map remembers progression, not a promise to restore every half-finished arrangement after a reload.

When something feels stuck

Rotate does nothing

Check the level first: rotation is intentionally locked in levels 1 and 2. On later levels, select a piece, release any active drag, then use Rotate. A square can look the same after turning because its outline is symmetric.

A piece keeps returning to its old position

It may overlap another piece, cross a hole, or sit partly outside the playable shape. Move more slowly until the preview aligns with the intended cells. If you want to remove a placed piece, move it clearly outside the puzzle until the removal feedback appears.

I need a calmer display

Open Settings and turn on Reduce motion. Sound effects and music can be changed separately. On a small screen, fullscreen may give you more room to see the outline. The detailed guide remains available from the map and the game’s side panel whenever you want to revisit a technique.
